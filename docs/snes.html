SNES games#

snes plays Super Nintendo (Super Famicom) games from ROM files, .sfc or .smc, in a window on the desktop:

snes "Super Game.sfc"
open "Super Game.sfc"     # the same, from anywhere (and from Firefox's downloads)
snes                      # choose a game from your files

On the classic desktop, SNES is on the Start menu and the desktop: it lists your folders and games to choose from (Enter or a double click plays one, Backspace goes up a folder, the mouse wheel scrolls, / finds a game in any folder below by typing parts of its name, as fzf does, and q quits).

The emulation is bsnes's: accurate to the cycle, with the cartridges' enhancement chips (Super FX, SA-1 and others), at full speed. It's the snes app, installed the first time you need it: snes and the desktop's SNES ask, or run sudo app install snes (about 5 MB to download, 22 MB installed). Lepton draws the pictures, as for the media player. The game's sound plays through the same mixer as Firefox's, at the system volume (or in Bluetooth headphones), so other sound goes on playing while a game runs.

The pad is read by the keys' places, so it's the same on every keyboard layout:

And for the emulator:

KeyDoes
P (or Pause)pause, and go on
Ctrl+Rreset the console
F5, F9save the game's state, load it back
1 to 9choose the state's slot first (slot 1 to start with)
F11 (or a double click)full screen, and back
Escleave full screen; in a window, close

Saves#

A game's own saves (the cartridge's battery RAM) are kept in ~/.local/share/snes, as GAME.srm, written a couple of seconds after the game saves and again when you close it, so you can go on where you left off. Save states (F5) go beside them, GAME.state1 to GAME.state9: a snapshot of the whole console, at any moment.

Game controllers#

USB and Bluetooth gamepads work, two at once for two players: the first one plugged in is player 1 (along with the keyboard), the second player

  1. Plug one in during a game and the title says which player it is.

The buttons go by their place, as on a SNES pad, whatever is printed on them: the bottom face button is B, the right one A, the left one Y and the top one X; the shoulder buttons (and triggers) are L and R; Select and Start are Select and Start. The D-pad, or the left stick, moves. The USB pads made to look like a SNES pad work as they're laid out.

Which pads: those that are ordinary USB or Bluetooth game controllers (most, including PlayStation pads, the F310 with its switch on D, and the SNES-style ones), and XInput pads: Xbox 360 pads and the many made like them, such as Logitech's F310 and F710 with their switch on X and 8BitDo's in X mode. Linux has a driver for XInput pads that Lepton's kernel doesn't include, so Lepton reads them from USB itself (built with the snes app's compiler, like the emulator's frontend). Xbox One and Series pads, and Nintendo's, aren't supported yet.

Limits#

The games themselves are yours to bring: Lepton doesn't come with any.

In a virtual machine the picture is at most three times the SNES's (768x672): full screen, or a bigger window, shows it that size in the middle, black around it. UTM's display stopped showing a bigger moving picture (the screen kept its old one while the game played on); on a computer of its own, the picture fills the screen. A picture bigger than the first window's also comes fewer times a second (about 40 at 3x), while the game itself runs at full speed.
