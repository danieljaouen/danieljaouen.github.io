Videos, music and radio#

Lepton plays videos and music with play, and internet radio with radio. Both are on the classic desktop's Start menu (Media Player, Radio) and its icons, and both work in a plain terminal too.

FFmpeg, once#

FFmpeg does the decoding: it reads nearly every format there is (MP4, MKV, WebM, MOV and AVI; MP3, AAC, FLAC, Ogg, Opus and WAV; HLS streams). It's an app, installed once, as root:

sudo app install ffmpeg

Or just open the Media Player or the Radio (or run play or radio): without FFmpeg, they ask whether to install it, and do, with your password. That's 110 packages from Alpine Linux, about 47 MB to download (110 MB installed). FFmpeg turns files into sound and pictures; Lepton does the rest itself: the sound goes to the sound card through Lepton's sound (so the system volume and the card you chose apply), and the pictures are drawn into a window on the desktop.

Videos and music: play#

play movie.mp4
play ~/Music/album
play --start 1:30 talk.webm

On the desktop, a video opens in a window, with a bar under it: the position, a progress line to click, the volume. In a terminal (or for music), a line shows what plays:

▶ [2/12] Nina Simone - Feeling Good  1:12 / 2:57 [========------------] vol 60%

Several files play one after another; a folder plays the media in it. play alone, in a terminal, lists the folders and media files where you are (or in ~/Videos, or ~/Music): Enter plays one and those after it. That's what the Start menu's Media Player opens.

Commands on this page#

CommandWhat it doesUsage
playthe media player: videos (in a window, on the desktop) and music, files or addresses (needs the ffmpeg app)play [--no-video] [--start TIME] [FILE|URL...]

The keys, in the terminal and in the window:

KeyWhat it does
Spacepause, play again
Left, Right5 seconds back or on
Up, Downa minute on or back
9, 0the volume down or up (m mutes)
n, pthe next or previous file (terminal)
f, double clickfill the screen (window; Esc returns)
q, Escstop (in the window: close it)

A click on the picture pauses; a click on the progress line goes there. When a video ends, its window stays, on its last picture: Space (or a click) plays it again; closing the window ends it, and play goes on to the next file. open video.mp4 opens a video (or music) in the Media Player too.

Internet radio: radio#

radio, in a terminal (or Radio, on the Start menu), shows your stations; a few listener-supported ones are there to begin with. Enter plays one; the song playing shows at the bottom, and changes as the next starts. Space stops it, and plays again.

/ searches Radio Browser's directory of about 50,000 stations, by name and by genre: try jazz, bbc or lofi. Tab switches between your stations and what was found; a adds the one chosen to yours, d removes one of yours.

From the command line:

radio groove                  # play one of yours (the start of its name)
radio search ambient          # what Radio Browser knows
radio add "Groove Salad" https://ice2.somafm.com/groovesalad-128-mp3
radio rm "Groove Salad"
radio list

Commands on this page#

CommandWhat it doesUsage
radiointernet radio: your stations, a search of 50,000 more, the song playing (needs the ffmpeg app)radio [STATION|URL] | list | search WORDS... | add NAME URL | rm NAME

Your stations are in ~/.config/lepton/radio, a name, a tab and an address a line; edit it as you like.

A station's stream is read the way radio players read it, asking for the titles that come with it (SHOUTcast and Icecast send each song's as it starts). Playlists (.pls, .m3u) and HLS streams work too. If a stream drops, radio opens it again.

When something doesn't play#

  • "FFmpeg isn't installed": sudo app install ffmpeg.
  • "no sound card": the pictures still play, without sound. In a virtual machine, add a sound device (see Sound).
  • "is in use": another program (Firefox, say) has the sound card. Stop it there.
  • Anything else: FFmpeg's words about it are in ~/.cache/lepton/ffmpeg.log.
