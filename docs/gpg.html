Encryption and signatures (gpg)#

gpg encrypts and signs with OpenPGP, as GnuPG does: the same commands and options, the same file names, the same messages. It's Lepton's own, written in Elixir, and it works with GnuPG both ways: keys move between them, and what one encrypts or signs, the other decrypts or verifies.

Your key#

gpg --quick-gen-key "Ada Lovelace <ada@example.com>"
gpg -k                              # your keys, and others'
gpg -a --export ada@example.com     # your public key, to give out

It asks for a passphrase to protect the key (on the terminal; nothing shows as you type). The key is an Ed25519 key for signing with a Curve25519 subkey for encryption, as GnuPG 2.4 makes them (--quick-gen-key UID rsa3072 makes RSA keys instead); it expires in three years unless you say otherwise (... default default never).

Keys are kept in ~/.gnupg (or $GNUPGHOME): pubring.gpg and secring.gpg. GnuPG keeps its own elsewhere: to bring a key over from a Mac, gpg -a --export-secret-keys you@example.com > key.asc there, and gpg --import key.asc here.

Others' keys#

gpg --import friend.asc
gpg --recv-keys 8C4B3AC2817BB4459DD0852AFF1DE4EF4EE9B36A   # keys.openpgp.org
gpg --locate-keys friend@example.com

There's no web of trust: a key you've imported is used, and its signatures show as [unknown] (yours as [ultimate]). Check a key's fingerprint (gpg -k NAME) with its owner before relying on it.

Encrypting and decrypting#

gpg -e -r friend@example.com report.pdf     # report.pdf.gpg
gpg -a -e -r friend@example.com notes.txt   # notes.txt.asc, text to paste
gpg -c secrets.txt                          # a passphrase instead of a key
gpg -d report.pdf.gpg > report.pdf

-r again adds recipients; add yourself to read it later too. Messages are AES-256 with an integrity check: a changed message isn't decrypted.

Signing and verifying#

gpg --clearsign notes.txt                  # notes.txt.asc: readable, signed
gpg -b release.tar.gz                      # release.tar.gz.sig, detached
gpg -s -e -r friend@example.com note.txt   # signed and encrypted
gpg --verify release.tar.gz.sig            # checks release.tar.gz

gpg: Good signature from "..." and status 0, or BAD signature and status 1.

Checksums: shasum#

shasum prints SHA checksums as macOS's does, and checks them:

shasum -a 256 lepton.iso
shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS

sha256sum, sha512sum, sha1sum and md5sum are there too.
