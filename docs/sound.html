Sound#

Lepton plays sound through the VM's sound device or a USB headset, speaker or dock. There is one volume for everything: Lepton's own sounds and Firefox alike.

volume              # Volume: 50% on HDA Intel
volume 75           # set it (0 to 100)
volume +10          # louder (volume up / volume down: 5 at a time)
volume mute         # mute (unmute, toggle)
sound test          # three notes, to check you can hear it
play song.wav       # play a WAV file (C-c stops it)
beep 440 500        # a 440 Hz tone for half a second

MP3, FLAC, Ogg and the rest, videos and internet radio: see Videos, music and radio (play and radio, with FFmpeg).

The volume and mute are remembered, on an installed system across restarts too.

On the desktop#

The volume keys on a keyboard (mute, volume down, volume up) work on the desktop, in Photon and the classic desktop.

On the classic desktop a speaker sits next to the clock. Click it for the volume control: click the slider to set the volume, and tick Mute to silence everything. The speaker shows a red cross while sound is muted. Click anywhere else to close the control.

Sound cards#

$ sound
  0  HDA Intel                    volume: Lepton Playback Volume
* 1  USB Headset                  volume: Lepton Playback Volume
(* plays sound: the card added last)

Sound plays on the card added last, so a USB headset takes over when you plug it in and hands back when you unplug it, and so do Bluetooth headphones when they connect (listed as bt). sound use 0 keeps sound on card 0 instead, sound use bluetooth on the headphones; sound use auto goes back to the one added last.

The volume works in software: each card gets a volume control of Lepton's (Lepton Playback Volume), and Lepton's sounds and Firefox's are made quieter, or silent when muted, before they reach the card. The card's own volume and mute are kept at full and on, because a virtual machine's sound card may not apply them: UTM's passes them on to UTM, which doesn't use them.

Drivers: Intel HD Audio (UTM's and QEMU's usual sound device), virtio-sound, and USB audio (headsets, speakers, sound cards, docks). They load when the device appears. Bluetooth headphones and speakers play through bluez-alsa; see Bluetooth.

In UTM#

Give the VM a sound device in its settings (Sound: Intel HD Audio, or VirtIO). Its sound comes out of your Mac. mix lepton.qemu gives its VM Intel HD Audio, played on the Mac's speakers too.

What it doesn't do yet#

  • Recording: microphones and line-in.
  • Mixing Lepton's own sounds with Firefox's: Firefox's tabs and windows play together, mixed, but while Firefox plays, beep and play may say the device is in use.
  • Files other than WAV for play (Firefox plays the web's formats).
  • Bluetooth headset microphones (headphones play; see Bluetooth).
