Bluetooth#

Lepton pairs and connects Bluetooth keyboards, mice and trackpads, headphones, headsets and speakers. A paired keyboard or mouse works like a USB one, at the console and on the desktop. Headphones become where sound plays.

bluetooth                         # on or off, and the paired devices
bluetooth scan                    # devices nearby (put yours in pairing mode first)
sudo bluetooth pair "MX Keys"     # pair, trust and connect (by name or address)
sudo bluetooth connect "MX Keys"
sudo bluetooth disconnect AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF
sudo bluetooth forget "MX Keys"   # unpair
sudo bluetooth off                # turn the radio off (and on)
bluetooth devices                 # every device Lepton knows of

Pairing, connecting and turning Bluetooth on or off change the system, so they take sudo. Scanning and looking don't.

Pairing#

Put the device in pairing mode (usually by holding its Bluetooth or connect button until a light blinks), then pair it. A device you paired reconnects by itself when you turn it on, after restarts too on an installed system.

  • Keyboards prove who they are by typing a number. Lepton shows it ("On MX Keys, type 482019 and press Enter"): type it on the Bluetooth keyboard itself and press Enter.
  • Mice and headphones usually pair without a question. Older ones that want a PIN are asked for it; 0000 is the usual one.
  • Some devices show a number and ask you to confirm it matches.

C-c stops pairing.

On the classic desktop#

With a Bluetooth adapter, the classic desktop's taskbar shows the Bluetooth rune next to the volume: blue when Bluetooth is on, grey when it's off. Click it for the devices: the connected ones (highlighted), the paired ones, and new ones nearby. Lepton looks for devices while the list is open.

Click a device to pair with it (a new one), connect it (a paired one) or disconnect it (a connected one). A small terminal window does it with the bluetooth command, which asks for your password if you aren't root, and for the keyboard's number while pairing. The button below the list turns Bluetooth off or on, or installs it the first time.

Headphones and speakers#

Connected headphones become where sound plays: Lepton's own sounds, play, beep, and Firefox. The volume command, the volume keys and the classic desktop's speaker set their volume. sound lists them; sound use 0 sends sound back to the sound card while they stay connected, and sound use bluetooth brings it back to them. They play in A2DP quality: there's no microphone (headset) mode.

What you need#

  • A USB Bluetooth adapter. A Mac's built-in Bluetooth belongs to macOS: in UTM, plug in a USB Bluetooth adapter, turn on Input → USB Sharing in the VM's settings, and pick the adapter from the USB icon in the toolbar. Most USB adapters work: Realtek and MediaTek ones (their firmware is included) and Broadcom ones. Intel adapters need firmware Lepton doesn't include.
  • Bluetooth support comes as an app: BlueZ (bluetoothd) with D-Bus, and bluez-alsa for headphones, from Alpine's packages (about 8 MB to download). The first sudo bluetooth on or pair installs it, as does the classic desktop's Install button or sudo app install bluetooth. It updates like Firefox: Lepton checks every ten days and applies an update the next time Bluetooth starts.

What it doesn't do yet#

  • Headset microphones and calls (HFP), file transfer (OBEX), phones and network sharing.
  • Bluetooth MIDI and game controllers' extra features.
