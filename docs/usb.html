USB, hubs and displays#

USB devices and hubs#

Plug things in directly or through a hub (or hubs of hubs) and they work the same way. The Linux kernel enumerates every hub's ports. When a device appears, Lepton loads its driver within a second:

DeviceDriverWhat you get
keyboards, mice, touchpads, tabletsusbhid and othersthey work at once (below)
USB sticks, drives and card readersusb-storage, uasmounted under /media (USB drives and SD cards)
Wi-Fi adapterssee Networkinga wireless interface
Ethernet ports (docks, adapters)r8152, ax88179_178a, asix, cdc_ether, cdc_ncman interface, configured with DHCP
USB display adaptersudl, gudanother display for the desktop (below)

usb (or lsusb) shows the devices as a tree. Each device is named by its port path: 1-1.2 is port 2 of the hub on port 1 of bus 1. Each line shows the device's ID, what it is, its speed and its driver. After the arrow is what the device became:

$ usb
usb1  xHCI Host Controller (root hub, 4 ports) [480 Mbit/s]
  1-1 0409:55aa  QEMU USB Hub (hub, 8 ports) [12 Mbit/s]
    1-1.1 0627:0001  QEMU USB Keyboard (HID) [12 Mbit/s; usbhid] -> event2
    1-1.2 0627:0001  QEMU USB Mouse (HID) [12 Mbit/s; usbhid] -> event4
    1-1.3 46f4:0001  QEMU USB HARDDRIVE (storage) [12 Mbit/s; usb-storage] -> sda
  1-2 0627:0001  QEMU USB Tablet (HID) [480 Mbit/s; usbhid] -> event1
  1-4 0525:a4a2  RNDIS/QEMU USB Network Device (communications) [12 Mbit/s; cdc_ether] -> usb0
usb2  xHCI Host Controller (root hub, 4 ports) [5 Gbit/s]

Keyboards and mice#

Any USB keyboard works, at the console and on the desktop (and so do Bluetooth ones, once paired). Mice, trackballs, touchpads, drawing tablets and touchscreens work on the desktop (Photon and the classic desktop). Plug them in at any time, directly, through a hub, or through a wireless receiver.

Most devices need only the standard HID driver. Lepton also has the drivers for Apple keyboards, Magic Mouse and Magic Trackpad, Logitech's Unifying and Bolt receivers, Microsoft, Lenovo, Cherry, ASUS and Elan devices, Wacom tablets and multi-touch touchpads, and loads them when one appears.

  • Mice: the wheel scrolls, a tilting or second wheel scrolls sideways, and every button (side buttons too) reaches the program under the pointer.
  • Touchpads work as on a Mac: one finger moves the pointer, a tap clicks (a two-finger tap right-clicks, a three-finger tap middle-clicks), two fingers scroll (up and down, and sideways), and pressing the pad clicks (with two fingers on it, a right click). Scrolling follows mouse scroll (natural by default). Laptops' built-in touchpads and touchscreens, which sit on an I2C bus rather than USB, work too: HID over I2C (most of them, described by ACPI or the device tree), and Elan, Cypress, Atmel and Synaptics ones. Their drivers, and those of the I2C controllers of Qualcomm, Rockchip, NVIDIA Tegra and Raspberry Pi machines, load at startup when the hardware is there. (A virtual machine has none: UTM and QEMU give a USB pointer.)
  • Tablets and touchscreens map their surface onto the display.
  • Keyboards: every key, with the US layout. The volume keys set the volume (see Sound).

input lists the input devices, what Lepton takes each for, and the USB port it's on:

$ input
event0   other     Power Button
event1   tablet    QEMU QEMU USB Tablet  (USB 1-2)
event2   keyboard  QEMU QEMU USB Keyboard  (USB 1-1.1)

At the text console a mouse does nothing (there is no console pointer).

In UTM, USB devices reach the VM one at a time. Turn on Input → USB Sharing in the VM's settings, then click the USB icon in the toolbar and choose a device. macOS keeps the hubs themselves, so a device behind a hub on your desk shows up in the VM as if it were plugged in directly. You rarely need to pass a keyboard or mouse: UTM already gives the VM a keyboard and pointer that follow your Mac's. A device you do pass belongs to the VM until you take it back, so keep another one for the Mac. Hubs show up as hubs on real hardware and in QEMU (mix lepton.qemu plugs its keyboard into a virtual hub).

Displays#

Photon (and the classic desktop) uses every display the kernel has a framebuffer for (/dev/fb0, /dev/fb1, ...):

  • a second display of the VM. In UTM, add a second Display device in the VM's settings and set its Emulated Display Card to virtio-gpu-pci (or virtio-gpu-gl-pci), not the default virtio-ramfb; UTM then opens a second window. With QEMU, run mix lepton.qemu --displays 2. A VM can have only one ramfb display (the firmware's framebuffer), and virtio-ramfb is a virtio GPU plus a ramfb. With two virtio-ramfb displays (or two ramfb), QEMU refuses to start the VM, before Lepton runs: duplicate fw_cfg file name: vgaroms/vgabios-ramfb.bin (or etc/ramfb). Keep the first display as it is and change the other one's card to virtio-gpu-pci.
  • a USB display adapter (passed to the VM like any USB device): adapters with DisplayLink's USB 2.0 chips (DL-120, DL-160, DL-165, DL-195) through the udl driver, and Generic USB Display gadgets (a Raspberry Pi acting as a monitor, for example) through gud.

Displays plugged in while the desktop runs join it within two seconds, and a display that goes away leaves it.

photon display           # the displays and how they're arranged
photon display extend    # one desktop across them, side by side (the default)
photon display mirror    # the same picture on every display

Extended (the default): the displays sit left to right. The first (/dev/fb0, where the console was) is the primary display, and the next one continues to its right. Move the pointer off the right edge, or drag a window there, to reach the next display. New windows open on the primary display, and maximizing fills the display the window's title bar is on. The classic desktop's taskbar and Start menu stay on the primary display. The desktop is as tall as the primary display. A taller display shows black below that, and a shorter one shows only the top part.

The pointer. A mouse moves across all the displays. The VM's own pointer (UTM's and QEMU's tablet) belongs to one display. UTM has one, for the first display. mix lepton.qemu --displays 2 gives each window its own. To move a window from the keyboard, press Alt+Shift+Right (or Left): the focused window moves to the next (or previous) display.

Mirrored: every display shows the primary display's picture, scaled to fit and centred, with black bars where the shapes differ.

HDMI on a USB-C hub or dock#

An HDMI port on a USB-C hub is one of two kinds, and they behave differently:

  • DisplayPort Alternate Mode, used by most USB-C hubs with HDMI. The picture comes from the computer's own graphics, carried on spare wires of the USB-C cable; it isn't a USB device. On a Mac, macOS drives that monitor and the VM can't take it over. Instead, drag UTM's window (or the VM's second display window) onto that monitor and make it full screen.
  • DisplayLink docks send the picture over USB, so the dock's display chip can be passed to the VM. Lepton drives DisplayLink's older USB 2.0 chips (udl). Newer ones (the DL-3000, DL-5000 and DL-6000 series in most USB 3 docks) need DisplayLink's closed-source driver, which Lepton doesn't have.
