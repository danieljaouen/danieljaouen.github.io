Languages and keyboards#

Accented letters (é ü ñ č) type everywhere with Alt, on the console as well as on the desktop: see Typing accents.

Caps Lock as Ctrl#

To make Caps Lock another Ctrl, as many Emacs and Vim users do (and as a Mac can, in System Settings: Keyboard, Keyboard Shortcuts, Modifier Keys):

keyboard caps ctrl

It works on the desktop, for Photon's shortcuts and every program, and on the text console, so it is the same on a PC with Lepton on its own disk as in a VM. The setting is the system's and is kept across reboots. keyboard caps lock makes it Caps Lock again, and keyboard caps says which it is.

In UTM, or over SSH, the keys come from the Mac: there, Caps Lock is whatever macOS makes it, so set it on the Mac as well.

Input sources#

The desktop types US English until you add other input sources: a keyboard layout (Thai, Hindi, Arabic, ...) or Korean. Add them with keyboard:

keyboard add ko th keyboard

* us  English (US)
  ko  Korean (Hangul, 2-set)
  th  Thai
Super+Space: the next one.
Korean: the Hangul key, Right Alt or Shift+Space switch between Hangul and English.

With more than one, Super+Space moves to the next source, and the classic taskbar shows the one in use (EN, KO, TH, ...) beside the clock: click it to move to the next. The choice is the system's, and is kept across reboots.

The layouts are xkeyboard-config's, the ones other Linux desktops use, with their plain and Shift characters. You can have up to three besides English. English stays the first layout, so shortcuts (Ctrl+C, Ctrl+T in Firefox) are where they always are, whatever you type with.

Korean#

Korean uses the US keys, with the 2-set layout printed on Korean keyboards: r is ㄱ, k is ㅏ, and Shift gives the doubled consonants (R is ㄲ) and ㅒ ㅖ. As you type, the jamo compose into a syllable, shown underlined while it can still change:

KeysText
gks한
gksrnrdj한국어
dkssudgktpdy안녕하세요

A vowel after a final consonant takes the consonant into the next syllable, as on any Korean keyboard (ekfr is 닭, ekfrk is 달가). Backspace takes back one jamo at a time. Space, Return, punctuation, a click or another window finish the syllable.

To type English without leaving Korean, press the Hangul key, Right Alt (where a Korean keyboard has its 한/영 key) or Shift+Space; the indicator shows A. Press it again for Hangul.

Hangul is composed in programs that take composed text: Firefox and other GTK programs, through Wayland's text-input protocol. In Lepton's own windows (terminal windows, le) the keys type English: they don't take composed text yet, though they show Korean text. To show Korean pages, Firefox comes with the fonts (see Apps).

In Lepton's own windows#

The layouts type in terminal windows too: the shell and le get the characters. Terminal windows, title bars and the taskbar draw with the built-in Terminus font, and with GNU Unifont's glyphs for what Terminus lacks: Chinese, Japanese and Korean, Thai, Devanagari, Arabic and the rest of Unicode's first 65,536 characters. They are bitmaps, one glyph per character: scripts whose letters join or reorder (Devanagari, Arabic) show their characters one by one, not shaped as Firefox shows them.

Chinese, Japanese and Korean characters take two columns, as in other terminals: the shell's line editor, le, ls and terminal windows count them so, and so do terminals you reach Lepton from over SSH.
