USB drives and SD cards#

Plug in a USB drive or stick, or insert an SD card (in a card slot or a USB card reader), and Lepton mounts it within a second or two, under /media. Its folder is named after the drive's label (/media/CAMERA), or after the device (/media/sdb1) when it has none.

lsblk                     # disks, partitions, filesystems and mount points
ls /media/CAMERA          # use the files like any others
eject /media/CAMERA       # ...then unplug it

Filesystems: FAT (FAT12, FAT16, FAT32: most sticks and SD cards), exFAT (large SD cards), NTFS (Windows drives), ext2/3/4 (Linux drives) and ISO 9660 (CD images written to a stick). FAT, exFAT and NTFS have no owners or permissions, so every user can read and write them. A filesystem the kernel won't write, such as an NTFS drive that Windows left hibernated, is mounted read-only.

Several partitions on a drive are each mounted in their own folder. A drive with no partition table, as many sticks and cards are, is mounted whole.

On the classic desktop, each mounted drive gets an icon below the others. Double-click it to open a terminal in the drive's folder.

Eject before you unplug#

Lepton writes to drives in the background, so pulling one out too soon can lose what you just saved. eject writes everything out, unmounts the drive and, for a USB drive, detaches it, so it's safe to remove. It isn't mounted again until you plug it back in.

eject                 # the only drive that is mounted
eject /media/BACKUP   # by folder (or any folder inside it)
eject sdb             # by device: every partition of the drive

eject fails while a program has a file on the drive open; close it first. A drive pulled out without ejecting is unmounted anyway, and its folder disappears.

A drive you unmount by hand (sudo umount /media/BACKUP) also stays unmounted until it's plugged in again. To mount one somewhere else, use sudo mount /dev/sdb1 /mnt/backup.

In UTM#

Turn on Input → USB Sharing in the VM's settings, then click the USB icon in the toolbar while the VM runs and pick the drive or card reader. macOS lets go of it and Lepton mounts it. To give the drive back to the Mac, eject it in Lepton first, then pick it again in the USB menu. See USB, hubs and displays.

Lepton's own disk (where an installed Lepton keeps your files) is never mounted under /media.
